Monitoring-Based Commissioning — Delivery Standard

Thornbury Energy Group · Version 3.0 · Effective 1 March 2026 · Owner: Marcus Oyelaran, Senior Commissioning Engineer · Supersedes v2.1 (June 2024)

1. Purpose and scope

This standard defines how Thornbury delivers Monitoring-Based Commissioning (MBCx) engagements. It applies to every paid MBCx contract and to the 24-month monitoring period included with every retrofit project.

MBCx exists because retrofit savings decay. A building commissioned to a verified baseline will typically give back between 15% and 30% of its measured savings within two years if nobody is watching — through overridden setpoints, altered schedules, failed sensors, and changes made by other trades. The purpose of MBCx is to detect that drift and correct it before the client notices it on a utility bill.

2. The proven process

Every engagement follows the same four stages. The stages are not optional and they are not reordered.

StageWhat it establishesExit criterion
Baseline What the building actually does now, measured rather than modeled A signed baseline model with at least 12 months of interval data, weather-normalised
Retrofit The physical and controls changes Installed, functionally tested, and accepted by the client
Verify That the savings are real and attributable Measured savings within the guarantee band for one full year
Monitor That they stay real Ongoing; quarterly tuning and an annual verification report

3. Baseline requirements

A baseline that cannot be defended is worse than no baseline, because it converts a savings guarantee into an argument. Minimum requirements:

  • Twelve months of interval data at no coarser than hourly resolution, from utility meters or a trusted building management system historian.
  • Weather normalisation against the nearest NOAA station with a complete record for the baseline year.
  • Documented exclusions. Any period excluded from the baseline — construction, an extended vacancy, a meter failure — is listed with dates and reason.
  • A recorded sequence of operations as found, not as designed. These differ in the large majority of buildings we survey.
  • Client countersignature on the baseline model before retrofit work begins. This is the single most effective dispute-prevention step in the process.

Every artifact listed in this section is filed to the knowledge base against the building, not to a project folder. A building outlives the project that created it, and it will outlive the engineer who commissioned it.

4. Monitoring period — what we actually do

4.1 Continuous

  • Automated fault detection on the points identified during commissioning.
  • Alarm on deviation from the verified sequence of operations.
  • Monthly energy performance against the weather-normalised baseline.

4.2 Quarterly tuning visit

  • Review of all faults raised since the last visit, with disposition for each.
  • Setpoint and schedule audit against the recorded sequence — this is where most drift is found.
  • Trend review of the five highest-consumption systems.
  • A written summary to the facilities lead within five business days.

4.3 Annual verification report

Measured savings for the year, weather-normalised, against the signed baseline. States plainly whether the guarantee band was met. Where it was not, states what we are doing about it and by when.

5. The savings guarantee

Thornbury guarantees measured first-year savings against the modeled figure in the signed baseline. If measured savings fall short, we true up the difference or continue working the building at no additional fee until they are met.

The guarantee is void only where the building's use changes materially from the baseline assumptions (occupancy, operating hours, process load), where the client or another contractor alters the commissioned sequence without notifying us, or where required maintenance in the operations and maintenance schedule is not performed. Each exclusion must be documented and notified within 30 days of discovery to be relied upon.

6. Staffing and continuity

Every MBCx engagement has a named lead engineer and a named second engineer. The second engineer is not a formality: they attend at least one tuning visit per year and are expected to be able to take the building over without a handover meeting.

This requirement was added in v3.0. Under v2.1, buildings were single-staffed, and the practical effect was that the company could not reassign an engineer without losing the building's history. Every building's baseline, sequence, fault history and tuning record is filed to the knowledge base so that the second engineer can be genuinely interchangeable.

7. Conversion from included monitoring

Every retrofit includes 24 months of monitoring at no additional charge. This is a commercial decision as much as a technical one: it is the demonstration that sells the paid contract.

At month 18, a named conversion owner is assigned and the following are prepared for the client conversation:

  1. Measured savings to date against the signed baseline.
  2. Every fault detected and corrected during the included period, with an estimate of the savings that would have been lost had it gone undetected.
  3. A drift projection for the building with no monitoring in place.
  4. A priced MBCx proposal, presented as protecting the capital already spent rather than as new spend.

Assignment of the conversion owner at month 18 is mandatory under v3.0. Under v2.1 conversion happened when the project engineer happened to have a good relationship with the facilities director, which converted six of twenty-three completions in FY2025.
