Quarterly Business Review — Q2 2026

Thornbury Energy Group · Period 1 April – 30 June 2026 · Presented 16 July 2026 · Attendees: Dana Thornbury, Marcus Oyelaran, Finance, Operations

1. Headline

Q2 closed at $7.62M revenue against a $7.9M plan — a 3.5% miss. Gross margin held at 37.1%. EBITDA came in at $737,000 (9.7%) against a plan of $903,000, an 18.4% shortfall, and net income at $439,000 (5.8%).

The revenue miss is small and explainable. The margin miss is the real finding, and it is almost entirely utilization.

A note on the runway figure. Our reporting pack computes runway as cash divided by gross monthly burn, which for Q2 gives 0.88 months. That number is close to meaningless for a business like ours — it treats every dollar of cost of goods sold as if no revenue were arriving to cover it. Net burn was negative $121,000 per month in Q2, meaning the company generated cash. The metrics we actually manage cash against are DSO and working capital, not runway.

3. Why revenue missed

Two retrofit projects slipped their construction windows out of Q2:

  • Harbor Pavilion (Seattle) — central plant replacement. The client's general contractor lost its mechanical subcontractor in late April. Our engineering was complete and accepted; we could not start commissioning without plant. Roughly $310,000 of planned Q2 revenue moved to Q3.
  • Willamette Commons (Portland) — envelope and controls. Permit revision required by the city added five weeks. About $190,000 moved to Q3.

Neither slip was caused by Thornbury and neither project is at risk. Both are expected to complete in FY2026.

4. Why margin missed — the utilization finding

Billable utilization for Q2 was 71.2% against a 72% target, on 47 billable FTEs and 17,770 direct labour hours delivered. On its own, 0.8 points under target is unremarkable.

What is not unremarkable is where the idle time sat. The two slipped projects took roughly eleven engineers to near-zero billable hours for between two and four weeks. Because the slips were not our fault, the projects stayed staffed and ready — and because they stayed staffed, those engineers were unavailable to be assigned elsewhere. We paid full cost for standby capacity and recovered none of it.

The agreed remedy

Operations proposed, and this review approved, an audit backfill rule:

  1. Any project slipping more than two weeks raises a backfill request within five business days.
  2. Affected engineers are assigned to energy audit work from the standing audit backlog for the duration of the slip.
  3. Audits are chosen from the backlog by pipeline value, so backfill work also feeds the retrofit funnel.

The audit backlog at the end of Q2 held eleven scoped opportunities worth roughly $640,000. This was judged more than sufficient to absorb foreseeable slippage.

Owner: Operations. First review: Q3 2026.

Top five clients were 56.4% of Q2 revenue, with Cascadia Health System at 19.4% on a trailing basis. The Cascadia master agreement goes to competitive rebid in Q1 2027 and preparation should begin no later than Q4 2026.

Founder dependency

41.5% of closed revenue in Q2 was closed personally by Dana. The FY2026 target is to be below 35% by December. The second senior seller role remains open.

6. Recurring revenue

MBCx contributed $907,000 in Q2, 11.9% of revenue. Trailing twelve months sits at approximately $3.24M, about 12.4%. The FY2026 exit target is 15%, and the three-year target is 28%.

Two retrofits completed their included 24-month monitoring period during Q2. One converted to a paid MBCx contract; one did not, and no conversion attempt was recorded. This is the gap the strategic plan identifies as the cheapest available growth.

7. Decisions taken

  1. Approve the audit backfill rule, effective immediately. Owner: Operations.
  2. Begin Cascadia rebid preparation in Q4 2026, not Q1 2027. Owner: Dana.
  3. Escalate the second senior seller hire; authorise an external recruiter. Owner: Dana.
  4. Restructure billing milestones onto engineering progress rather than construction completion for all new contracts. Owner: Finance.
  5. Assign a named owner to every retrofit reaching the end of its included monitoring period. Owner: Marcus.
