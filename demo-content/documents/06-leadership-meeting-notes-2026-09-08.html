Leadership Team — Meeting Notes

Thornbury Energy Group · Monday 8 September 2026, 08:00–09:15, Seattle · Present: Dana Thornbury, Marcus Oyelaran, Operations, Finance · Notes: Operations · Internal — not for distribution outside the leadership team

1. Q3 utilization — the backfill rule did not happen

Operations opened with the number: billable utilization for Q3 to date is 66.4%, against the 72% target and against 71.2% in Q2. On 48 billable FTEs this is the worst quarter in two years.

The Q2 review approved an audit backfill rule in July precisely to stop this. It has been triggered twice since and executed zero times.

Operations walked through what actually happened:

  • Harbor Pavilion slipped a second time in mid-July when the replacement mechanical subcontractor rescheduled. Backfill request raised 20 July, on time and per the rule.
  • Riverbend Logistics pushed its controls cutover to October at the client's request. Backfill request raised 6 August.
  • Both requests reached the audit backlog and then stopped. Neither audit was scoped.

The reason is that scoping an audit requires Dana's sign-off on the fee and the approach before it can be assigned. Dana has been almost entirely committed to Cascadia rebid preparation since late July — which the Q2 review itself brought forward to Q4, and which has in practice started earlier than that.

Marcus put it plainly and it is recorded here because it is the honest version: the remedy for the utilization problem was routed through the single busiest person in the company, so the remedy inherited the founder-dependency problem. Roughly nine engineers carried significant idle time across July and August as a result.

Finance confirmed the cost: the Q3 gross margin is tracking at 36.3% against 37.1% in Q2, and EBITDA at $312,000 (5.2%) on $5.98M of revenue to 22 September — against $737,000 (9.7%) in Q2. Net income is $116,000, a 1.9% margin. Most of that deterioration is paid-for capacity that was not billed.

Decision. Audit scoping authority is delegated. Marcus may approve audit scope and fee up to $75,000 without Dana. Above that, Dana signs. Effective immediately, retroactive to the two outstanding requests, both to be scoped and assigned this week.

Owner: Marcus. Due: 12 September.

Dana accepted the finding without argument and noted that this is the third time in eighteen months that an operational fix has stalled on founder availability. Agreed that the pattern, not the instance, is the thing to fix.

3. Cash

DSO has moved the wrong way: 68 days, up from 61 in Q2, against a 45-day target. Receivables past 90 days are $1.9M. Cash is $1,840,000 against $2,140,000 at the end of Q2.

Finance reported that the Q2 decision to move billing milestones onto engineering progress has been applied to new contracts only — it cannot be applied retroactively, so the aged balance is working through on the old terms. Two clients account for $1.1M of the over-90 balance; both are Harbor Pavilion-related and both are disputing nothing. It is a paperwork backlog on their side.

Action. Finance to escalate both over-90 accounts to the client's AP directly rather than through the project manager. Due: 19 September.

4. Sales

Q3 to date: 147 leads, 412 calls, 34 proposals issued, 7 won — a 20.6% conversion rate, down from 28.2% in Q2. Deal velocity has lengthened from 88 to 94 days. Pipeline coverage is 1.72×, down from 1.94×.

Dana attributed both the conversion drop and the velocity increase to the same cause as section 1: time spent on Cascadia rebid preparation is time not spent closing. Founder-closed revenue is 43% this quarter, up from 41.5% — moving away from the under-35% target rather than toward it.

The second senior seller role has been open since March. The external recruiter authorised in July has produced four candidates, two of whom withdrew. Average time to fill across our nine open roles is now 84 days.

5. MBCx

Marcus reported three retrofits reaching the end of their included 24-month monitoring window in Q4. All three now have a named conversion owner, which is new. MBCx revenue in Q3 to date is $741,000, 12.4% of revenue.

Marcus also noted the knowledge base is now carrying the building histories for all three, which made assigning a conversion owner who had not worked the original project possible for the first time. Previously that handover would have required the original engineer.
